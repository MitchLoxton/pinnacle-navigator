# Benchmark 13 — Blake Ryan / “YouTube's New Content Format…”

Source video: `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GzhT10i4vag`

Purpose: this is the human-quality benchmark for what a premium YTIntel Analyse result should feel like. It is based on the supplied 14-page benchmark pack dated 6 Sep 2026 and is intentionally more demanding than a transcript summary.

## 1. Short version

The video is a roughly 9-minute talking-head-over-screen-recording pitch. Blake uses a 13.8% US TV viewing statistic to argue that ultra-long YouTube content is the next opportunity, then shows long AI-made recap channels, revenue claims and his own software/coaching funnel as proof.

The real creator lesson is not “copy the format.” The useful transferable mechanics are: proof before persuasion, a correction re-hook, screen-recorded arithmetic, named third-party cases, a physical mechanism for an abstract thesis, objection pre-emption and title testing.

## 3. First-minute analysis

A premium output should explain what each early line is doing, not simply quote it.

- **0:00** — “secret plan” + promise to prove it. Function: insider framing plus open loop.
- **0:06** — recency/authority framing around “news” and an annual review. Function: gives the claim urgency even though the source is vague.
- **0:13** — 13.8% US TV viewing stat shown on screen. Function: pays the evidence promise within the first 15 seconds.
- **0:23** — corrects a likely misreading of the stat before the viewer voices it. Function: strong re-hook and comprehension reset.
- **0:42** — emotional stamp. Function: escalates significance.
- **0:49–0:58** — revenue dashboard and automated-channel claim. Function: receipt before mechanism; the viewer sees money before being asked to believe the thesis.

Useful timing targets from the benchmark:
- first receipt: about 0:54
- first “what this means for creators” payoff: about 1:50
- first explicit sell: about 7:21

## 6. Claims ledger behavior

A premium YTIntel output should classify important claims instead of repeating them as facts.

Examples from this benchmark:

- 13.8% of US TV viewing: real historical figure, but stale versus the latest month at the time.
- “came out 3 days ago” / annual YouTube review: wrong framing.
- “13% around the world”: wrong; the displayed figure was US-specific.
- $3,400 on 129k views / about $30 RPM: creator claim with arithmetic inconsistency.
- Harry Potter lore $30 RPM estimate: weak/unverified assumption.
- phone-to-TV controls as proof YouTube wants long videos: weak evidence.
- recap-channel $25 RPM and $3.6M estimate: arithmetic works, premise not verified.
- software/client revenue claims: conflict-of-interest / unverifiable.
- “unlimited” ElevenLabs claim: overstated relative to a capped product allowance.

The important product behavior is the verdict per claim: verified, stale, wrong, estimate, unsupported, conflict-of-interest, etc.

## 11. Cross-channel validation

The benchmark’s cross-channel research found that Blake’s exact “13.8% TV share → make ultra-long AI recaps” framing had weak independent signal, while a neighboring topic — YouTube Shows / TV-native episodic packaging — had stronger evidence from other creator-tool channels.

The general product rule: separate the creator’s framing from the actual market vein.

## 12. Business / funnel read

The video functions as a lead-generation funnel:

stat → urgency → own revenue receipt → outside cases → pre-AI cost pain → software solution → CTA → objection handling → CTA again.

The benchmark estimates about 31% of runtime is selling. That should be surfaced because views may not be the creator’s true business KPI; booked calls or software/coaching conversions may be.

## 13. What to steal / what not to copy

### Transferable

- receipt-first skeleton
- correction re-hook
- arithmetic on screen
- physical object/mechanism for abstract claim
- objection pre-emption
- multiple title variants
- fixed upload timing

### Do not blindly copy

- unverified revenue numbers
- quantity-over-quality doctrine
- borrowed-IP mass-production model
- vague “secret plan” authority framing without sources
- oversized guru-funnel sales share if it conflicts with the user’s brand

## 14. Remake behavior

A premium YTIntel remake should be built from the current user/channel rules and evidence, not from generic title formulas. The benchmark proposed multiple distinct directions, each with source-backed hooks, thumbnail concepts, structure and honest caveats.

## 15. Measurement plan

After publishing a remake, YTIntel should tell the creator what to measure, such as:
- title test winner
- retention at the re-hook / first receipt
- device type / TV share where relevant
- average view duration by chapter for ultra-long formats
- comment response to the core claim
- Most Replayed once available

## 16. Regression failures called out by this benchmark

The supplied benchmark explicitly identifies these old YTIntel failures:

- subscribers shown as 0 instead of about 6,190
- WPM shown as 692.7 instead of 230.4
- no pull-time context on views
- missing channel median and outlier multiple
- no explanation/fallback when Most Replayed is unavailable
- failure to detect concurrent title testing
- slow/broken surfaces that simply spin or stop without saying which dependency failed

## Acceptance statement

For this exact video, an Analyse output is only “premium” if it demonstrates genuine synthesis across the whole runtime. Restating caption lines as separate takeaways is a fail even if the transcript itself is accurate.
